# Liza Platonov

As a kid, I had a fairly complicated relationship with math. My dad is a high school math teacher, and he was very committed to making sure that I had a strong foundation in math. For most of elementary and middle school, my dad and I would do between 30 minutes and two hours of supplementary math every day at home. He also had some “unconventional teaching methods,” typically pushups and situps. When I reached high school, I finally got some reprieve from the at-home math lessons, and I started to adopt a more casual attitude toward the subject. In college, I immediately committed to a computer science major, but math slowly started to creep back in, and junior year I ended up adding a math major. 

I’m so glad that I found the math department in the end. It’s been really rewarding to me to be able to use the tools that I’ve been building up since elementary school in much more complex applications. One of my favorite things about math is the way that everything is connected. Every time I take a new class or learn about a new concept I can find ways that it relates to something else I have learned. I may have joined the math major relatively late in my Middlebury career, but I’ve learned so much and I’m very grateful for the community I have in the math department. I’ve made so many great friends through the math department and the way that the math students here always help each other out has really contributed to my love for my math major. Next year, I’m starting a math PhD at Univeristy of Utah, and I’m forever grateful to the Middlebury math department for helping me discover my interest in math.
